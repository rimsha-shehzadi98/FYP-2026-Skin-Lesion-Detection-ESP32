Introduction

Skin diseases such as Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) and Nevus are among the most prevalent dermatological conditions worldwide, requiring early and accurate diagnosis for effective treatment. Conventional detection relies on clinical specialist visits that remain inaccessible in rural and resource-limited areas. This project presents a real-time embedded AI system deployed on the low-cost ESP32-CAM microcontroller, capable of detecting BCC and Nevus directly at the edge without any cloud dependency. A FOMO MobileNetV2 model trained on 2,800 labelled dermoscopic images was deployed via a custom Wi-Fi web dashboard that captures images, runs 534 ms on-device inference, and displays results with confidence scores — making early skin disease screening affordable and accessible for all communities.

Objectives
  • To design and deploy a real-time skin lesion detection system on a low-cost ESP32-CAM embedded device without cloud dependency.
  • To train a FOMO MobileNetV2 model capable of accurately classifying BCC and Nevus skin disease categories from dermoscopic images.
  • To develop a Wi-Fi web dashboard providing live camera preview, sequential image capture, and real-time result visualization.
  • To evaluate system performance using F1 Score, confidence metrics, confusion matrix, and real-time inference timing.

Conclusion
  • A fully functional embedded AI system was successfully developed and deployed on the ESP32-CAM for real-time skin disease detection of BCC and Nevus — with no cloud dependency, making it accessible in resource-limited settings.
  • The FOMO MobileNetV2 model achieved an overall F1 score of 83.9%, with Nevus attaining the highest confidence of 88.4% and F1 of 0.86, while BCC achieved 80.4% confidence and F1 of 0.82 — within a 534 ms inference window.
  • The Wi-Fi web dashboard provides live camera streaming, 12-image capture, confidence pie chart, detection timeline, confidence trend graph, and CSV export — all without specialist hardware.
  • Future work includes dataset expansion with Melanoma and Squamous Cell Carcinoma, low-light image enhancement, and development of a dedicated Android mobile application.
